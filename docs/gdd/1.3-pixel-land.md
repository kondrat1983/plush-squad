# v1.3 Pixel Land: design doc

World 6 of Plush Squad: an old video game cartridge where everybody, Jack and the player's own toys included, turns into 8-bit pixels. Rivals Sir Leafy, Bolty, Bibi, Pipo; boss the **Pixel Dragon**. Written by `game-designer` on 4 Nov 2026 (updated the same day with the owner's answers) from the code on `main` (v1.2.3, commit 64210a7). Owner decisions of 4 Nov 2026 are in `decisions.md`.

Rules that apply: a new world is harder than the one before; every boss gives a hat; one comic per world; toy and friend duels scale with level; bedtime and the daily limit apply; Kraken stays at 220; hotfixes never go into What's new. Kid-safe: nobody gets hurt, rivals giggle and give up. **The four rivals are affectionate parodies of old console heroes. They must be recognisable by their job and their gags, not by copied names, faces, outfits, catch phrases, music or sounds.** No fake accents.

---

## 1. Pitch

Jack pokes an old game cartridge and falls inside. The world is made of squares, the music goes beep-boop, and Jack becomes a hand-drawn 8-bit Jack and any toy he brings is turned into 8-bit pixels automatically, after a "LOADING..." screen. Four old-school heroes want a pillow fight: a silent sword-and-shield elf knight, a blue blaster robot, a bubble-blowing little dino and a moustached plumber. The boss is the **Pixel Dragon**: a real red dragon from an old cartridge, who has never met a dragon made of plush. Two dragons, one pillow fight.

The new rule is **1-UP**: once per duel a chest pops open and a pixel heart drops. Whoever grabs it first on their turn heals and scores. The boss rule is **CONTINUE?**: when the Pixel Dragon runs out of pep, a "GAME OVER" countdown starts and you must tap NO before he continues.

Why a 10-year-old cares: his own toy becomes an 8-bit hero (a photo of his own plush, in NES colours), the music is a real chiptune, and the heart is a small race against the rival that makes him think about when to heal instead of attack. Why a 6-year-old does too: "LOADING..." and squares are funny, and on EASY the rival never takes the heart.

## 4. Signature mechanic: 1-UP

**One new rule. Once per duel a chest opens and a pixel heart drops. Whoever claims it first, on their own turn, heals and scores. Claiming costs the turn.**

### Flow
1. **The chest.** From the start of the duel a closed pixel chest sits on the ground between the fighters (centre, behind both, 112 px). It wobbles every 4 s ("something is in there").
2. **It opens.** At duel start the game draws a turn number `K` (3 or 4) and a side (50% the player, 50% the rival), stored in `snapshot()`. After turn `K` of that side the lid flips open, `chest` sound, the heart pops out (64 px, bounces, blinks 2 frames) with a `1-UP` pixel label. The side that did **not** just act has **first chance**: the chest opens at the end of the rival turn = you choose first; it opens at the end of your turn = the rival chooses first.
3. **Two chances.** The first chooser decides at the start of their next turn, the second one at the start of theirs. If both pass, the heart floats up and away (`bloop`). It is never taken back and never comes twice.
4. **Never mid-charge.** The chest waits (opens one turn later) while the rival is charging (Fire Breath), dizzy, or the hero is dizzy; it also never opens when either fighter is below 15% pep [?, so a heal-to-survive is never a trap].

### Claiming
| | Player | Rival (AI rule) |
|---|---|---|
| How | tap the heart (160 px hit area) **or** the `1-UP!` button (400 x 130, where BLOCK IT! sits, pulsing); costs the turn, like PUSH! and ALARM! | decided at the start of its turn, replaces its move |
| Heal | EASY +30, NORMAL +25, HARD +20 pep (not scaled by level, capped at max pep) | EASY: never claims. NORMAL: 12% of its max pep (22-31, rounded to 5). HARD: 12% |
| When | any time it is up on your turn | NORMAL: always if its pep < 60%, else 35% of the time. HARD: always if pep < 80%, else 60%. Both: never if it can finish the hero this turn (hero pep <= its smallest hit) |
| Stats | `stats.oneups` +1 | `stats.rivalOneups` +1 (for the QA only) |

- **EASY:** the rival never takes the heart ("looks at the heart... and forgets it!" once in the log). The heart waits for two of your turns.
- **Scoring:** arcade flavour, no penalty. Score = damage Jack dealt x 10 + 1000 per 1-UP claimed + pep left x 10 on a win (+2000 on the boss if no CONTINUE? happened). Shown as `SCORE 004250` (6 digits, 34 px) top centre under the VS badge, clear of the emoji button and the pep bars (QA checks overlaps on D1-D5). The result panel shows the score and `HI-SCORE!` (blinking) when it beats `Save.data.hi[rivalId]`. Only wins count. [?: hi-score is new save data, see question 5.]
- **Message:** the heart flies to the claimer's pep bar, the bar flashes, a pixel `+25` pops, `oneup` jingle (rival: the same jingle one octave lower, `oneupRival`). Log: `Jack grabs the 1-UP! +25 pep!` / `{rival} dives for the 1-UP! Aw, too slow!`
- **First 1-UP ever** (`stats.heartSeen`): a hand icon on the heart and the log line `Tap the heart before the rival does!`.

### Why it is fair and fun
It is a small race: the kid sees the heart and has to decide to heal now or keep hitting; the rival is a visible competitor on NORMAL and HARD. It gives a 6-year-old a free-feeling heal (EASY: always his) and a 10-year-old a real decision (HARD: the rival often takes it first, so tap early). Nobody is hurt.

## 4b. Boss mechanic: CONTINUE? (Pixel Dragon)

Old arcade games ended with `GAME OVER. CONTINUE? 9 8 7...`. The Pixel Dragon wants to continue.

1. **First KO only** (`R.continues = 1`). When his pep reaches 0 the first time, he does not giggle yet: he flashes, `GAME OVER` in big pixel letters (`gameover` sound, 0.9 s), then `CONTINUE?` with a countdown **9 to 0** in the middle of the arena, ticking with a `tick` blip.
2. **You tap NO!** A big `NO!` button (400 x 130, where BLOCK IT! sits) pulses. Tapping it before 0: `NO CONTINUES!`, `levelUp` jingle, he giggles and gives up (normal win). `stats.noContinue` +1.
3. **You wait or miss.** At 0 he says `CONTINUE!`, pops back up with a `x1` chip, **20% of his max pep** (NORMAL 52, HARD 25% = 80) and a short gold flash. The next KO ends the duel at once (no second prompt). The duel goes on as normal.
4. **Never mean:** no damage to Jack, no turn lost, nothing taken away. The only cost of missing is one more small bar of pep to beat.

|---|---|---|---|

## 4c. The look: hand-made sprites and an automatic filter for toys (campaign duels in Pixel Land only)

### Frames (Jack and every rival)

### Automatic filter `pixelize(scene, srcKey)` for photo toys (new, 30-50 lines)
Reads the Phaser texture's source image, never writes to it. The strict NES palette snap was tried and looked bad in tests, so it is dropped: the palette is "NES-like" (limited, saturated), not snapped.

| Step | What | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Quantize at full size | free quantize of the whole picture | **12 colours** |
| 2. Resize | `mode` resize (the most common colour in each cell, keeps eyes and small details) | about **64-72 px tall**, width proportional |
| 3. Alpha | hard edges, alpha >= 128 becomes opaque | no soft edges |
| 4. Outline | 1 art pixel, dark `#101830`, 4-neighbour | canvas grows by 2 px each way |
| 5. Upscale | nearest, x4, then `addCanvas('px:toy_<id>:<rev>')` and `setFilter(NEAREST)` | cached |
- A toy has no frames: the squash tween stays, and the `hit` frame is a short white flash.
- **Cache:** key `px:<textureKey>` plus a revision for the photo (`toy.pic` stamp or the length of the stored data URL [?]); LRU of 24; a rotation rebuild pixelates again (about 20 ms), with no boot animation.
- **Never changes saved pictures:** it reads the in-memory texture and writes only `px:*` textures. It never touches `IDB`, `Save.data.toys` or `toy_<id>`, so Squad, Me, Studio, mail and friends always show the real picture. Test: the IDB blob of a toy is byte-equal before and after a Pixel Land duel.
- **GOLDEN toys:** the gold copy is built from the pixel key (a hard gold block).
- **Performance (iPhone SE class):** one or two pixel canvases per duel (the toy, the friend's toy), about 70 x 90 art px each, total time **<= 60 ms**, run once at duel start, never per frame; no WebGL filter or post-processing; memory under 100 KB. `__psPixMs` exposes the time.
- **Failure:** if canvas or `addCanvas` throws, the duel runs with the normal toy texture and still plays the boot screen. Test with `__psPixFail = true`.
- **Particles:** the round `dot` becomes the 4 px square `sqdot` in this world; counts unchanged.

### Palette
"NES-like": a limited set of saturated colours, about 12 per sprite, a dark outline `#101830` and no gradients. The hand-made sprites use the same set so toys and rivals sit together. The art list (section 7) names the hex values actually used once the owner approves the sheets; the doc does not fix a 54-colour table any more.

## 7. Audio and look

### Music: `pixel` (duels) and `pixelMap` (the map)
Original NES-style chiptune, no tune quoted. Added to `A.TRACKS` like the others; the map version comes from the existing `forEach` (80% tempo, no drums, gain x0.75, `soft`).
- **Key and speed:** G major with a minor turn in the B part (E minor), 4/4, **150 bpm**, `div: 4`, `len: 256` (16 bars), `gain` about 0.6.
- **Channels (like the NES: 2 pulse + triangle + noise):**
  - Pulse 1 (lead): **50% duty**, melody in 8th and 16th notes, short staccato decay, `vol` 0.03.
  - Pulse 2 (echo and arps): **25% duty**, 16th-note arpeggio on the chord, one octave up, `vol` 0.014; the B part answers the lead 3 steps late (echo, as `space`).
  - Triangle bass: root, fifth, octave in 8ths on `triangle`, `vol` 0.12 (no filter).
  - Noise "drums": `nkick` (a triangle drop 150 to 45 Hz in 80 ms), `nsnare` (noise burst 70 ms, bandpass 2.2 kHz), `nhat` (noise 25 ms, highpass 8 kHz). All use `A.hit` / `A.voice`, so `soft` removes them on the map.
- **New helper:** `A.pulse(m, at, dur, duty, o)` in `audio.js`: `voice()` only supports `sine / square / sawtooth / triangle`, so the pulse waves come from a `PeriodicWave` for duty `d` (Fourier terms `b_n = (2 / (n*PI)) * sin(n*PI*d)`, cached per duty, 32 harmonics). A third duty of 12.5% is allowed for the boss chiptune.
- **Structure:** A (bars 1-8): lead tune over I V vi IV with a 2-bar turnaround; B (bars 9-16): minor, lead moves up an octave, snare roll on bar 16. No lead note longer than 4 beats (chiptune feel). A test in `music.spec.js` checks `pixel` and `pixelMap` exist and that `WORLD_TRACK[6] === 'pixel'`.
- **Where:** the 4 rivals: `pixel`; the map: `pixelMap`; the Pixel Land comic: `calm`; the Pixel Dragon: `boss` (like every crowned boss). Optional last PR: a faster chiptune `pixelboss` (C minor, 168 bpm, 12.5% duty lead, pitch-bend on bar 8), chosen through `R.track` in `Battle.track()`.
- Everything is synthesized in `audio.js`: no audio files.
